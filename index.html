Application of Derivatives

Maxima & Minima Optimization Problems

Optimization involves finding the maximum or minimum value of a function. Derivatives give us the exact rate of change—setting $f'(x) = 0$ reveals the critical points where extreme values occur.

Interactive 3-Sided Garden Optimizer

Adjust total fence length to maximize the enclosed area against a straight wall.

60m

Mathematical Derivation

1. Setup Constraint: $2x + y = 60 \implies y = 60 - 2x$

2. Area Function: $A(x) = x \cdot y = x(60 - 2x) = 60x - 2x^2$

3. Take Derivative: $A'(x) = 60 - 4x$

4. Critical Point ($A'(x)=0$): $4x = 60 \implies x = 15\text{ m}$

5. Second Derivative Test: $A''(x) = -4 < 0 \implies \text{Confirmed Maximum!}$

Optimal Width ($x$) 15 m
Optimal Length ($y$) 30 m
Max Area ($A$) 450 m²
1

Find Critical Points

Take the first derivative $f'(x)$, set it to $0$, and solve for $x$. These identify flat stationary points where the graph turns.

2

1st Derivative Test

Check the sign of $f'(x)$ on either side of critical point $c$:

  • $+$ to $-$ : Local Maximum
  • $-$ to $+$ : Local Minimum
3

2nd Derivative Test

Evaluate $f''(c)$ at critical point $c$:

  • $f''(c) < 0$ : Concave down $\implies$ Maximum
  • $f''(c) > 0$ : Concave up $\implies$ Minimum
Fundamental Rules

Rules for Finding Derivatives

Master the foundational rules of differentiation: Power Rule, Product Rule, Quotient Rule, and the Chain Rule for composite functions.

Power Rule

Basic
$$\frac{d}{dx}[x^n] = n \cdot x^{n-1}$$

Bring the exponent to the front and decrease the power by 1.

Example: $\frac{d}{dx}[5x^4] = 5 \cdot (4x^3) = 20x^3$

Product Rule

Combination
$$\frac{d}{dx}[u \cdot v] = u \cdot v' + v \cdot u'$$

"First times derivative of second plus second times derivative of first."

Example: $\frac{d}{dx}[x^2 \cdot e^x] = x^2 e^x + e^x (2x) = x e^x (x + 2)$

Quotient Rule

Fractions
$$\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{u}{v}\right] = \frac{v \cdot u' - u \cdot v'}{v^2}$$

"Low d-high minus high d-low, over the square of what's below."

Example: $\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{x}{x+1}\right] = \frac{(x+1)(1) - x(1)}{(x+1)^2} = \frac{1}{(x+1)^2}$

Chain Rule

Composite
$$\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$$

Derivative of outside function evaluate inside, multiplied by derivative of inside.

Example: $\frac{d}{dx}[(3x+1)^5] = 5(3x+1)^4 \cdot (3) = 15(3x+1)^4$

Trigonometric Rules & Visualizer

Function vs Derivative Grapher

Observe how the tangent slope of $f(x)$ perfectly traces $f'(x)$.

Sine

$$\frac{d}{dx}[\sin(x)] = \cos(x)$$

Cosine

$$\frac{d}{dx}[\cos(x)] = -\sin(x)$$

Tangent

$$\frac{d}{dx}[\tan(x)] = \sec^2(x)$$

Cosecant

$$\frac{d}{dx}[\csc(x)] = -\csc(x)\cot(x)$$

Secant

$$\frac{d}{dx}[\sec(x)] = \sec(x)\tan(x)$$

Cotangent

$$\frac{d}{dx}[\cot(x)] = -\csc^2(x)$$
Advanced Derivatives

Higher-Order & Implicit Differentiation

Learn to compute second, third, and $n$-th derivatives, as well as finding $\frac{dy}{dx}$ for equations where $y$ cannot be explicitly isolated.

Higher-Order Derivatives

Taking the derivative of a derivative yields higher-order functions. For motion: Position $s(t) \to$ Velocity $v(t) = s'(t) \to$ Acceleration $a(t) = s''(t)$.

1st Derivative: $f'(x) = \frac{dy}{dx}$
2nd Derivative: $f''(x) = \frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$
3rd Derivative: $f'''(x) = \frac{d^3y}{dx^3}$
Worked Example:

Find $f''(x)$ for $f(x) = x^4 - 3x^2 + 5$

• $f'(x) = 4x^3 - 6x$

• $f''(x) = 12x^2 - 6$

Implicit Differentiation

When $x$ and $y$ are intermingled (e.g. $x^2 + y^2 = 25$), differentiate both sides with respect to $x$, applying the Chain Rule to $y$ terms (multiply by $\frac{dy}{dx}$).

Worked Circle Example:

Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ for $x^2 + y^2 = 25$

1. Differentiate w.r.t $x$: $2x + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$

2. Solve for $\frac{dy}{dx}$: $2y\frac{dy}{dx} = -2x$

3. $\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{2x}{2y} = \mathbf{-\frac{x}{y}}$
